Why it matters now

Transversal skills are in the curriculum. Grades cannot show them.

Finland's core curricula make transversal competence part of every subject, from thinking and learning to learn to working life competence and entrepreneurship. At the same time, schools and municipalities are under pressure to reverse falling results, and to show that their teaching builds problem-solving and resilience, not just rote memorisation.

−79 points

is how far Finland's PISA mathematics score has fallen since its 2006 peak: from 548 to 469 in PISA 2025.

1 in 4

Finnish 15-year-olds were already below the baseline level in mathematics in PISA 2022. In PISA 2025 that share rose again, more steeply than across the OECD on average, and gaps between students keep widening.

41%

of Finnish students say they put in extra effort when work becomes challenging: the smallest share of all 91 countries and economies in PISA 2025.

Final grades

and completed tasks are what platforms like Wilma are built to record. Transversal skills are left to subjective observations, manual spreadsheets or self-rating forms, so no one can see at scale whether an intervention is working.

A student who struggles with a hard maths problem sees one thing: a low score. The persistence, teamwork and trial-and-error that went into it stay invisible, and end-of-term feedback like “try harder” does not say what to do next. Grades show the destination. FEONA shows the journey.

How it works in the classroom

Four steps inside a normal course, and none of them ask students to rate themselves.

01

Logic model

Start from the change you want: the transversal skills to build and the levels to aim for. Pre-built logic models link each lesson, task or project to those skills, so every activity has a clear line of sight to an intended outcome.

02

Observation

Students work on everyday tasks and real-world challenges, from a maths problem solved by trial and error to a project for someone outside the class. The teacher records what they actually do against a novice-to-expert rubric, during the work rather than afterwards. Peer defense, where students present and defend their work to classmates, adds evidence on both sides.

03

Skills Map

Each piece of evidence appears on the student's personal Skills Map while the course is still running: the problem-solving, resilience and teamwork they showed, the gaps that remain and a concrete next step. An attempt that did not work still produces evidence, so trying is never wasted.

04

Validation and impact

When the course ends, the evidence is validated into a record of development. Principals and education directors see the same evidence across classes and schools: which teaching approaches are building which skills, and where students need a different kind of support.

Teachers are trained and certified. The evidence is reliable because the people who record it are calibrated, not because of the software. Training for teachers is part of SkillsForge, not an extra.

Not a grade, and not a ranking. SkillsForge gives no total score and never ranks students against each other. Gaps in the evidence are shown as gaps, never as a zero. It complements grades and the teacher's own assessment; it does not replace them.

Students

Teachers

Built-in logic models

A clear line of sight from classroom task to skill.

Mapping daily tasks to a competence framework by hand takes hours, so it rarely happens. SkillsForge scaffolds the logic model, a theory of change for your teaching, and automates the link between every activity and the skills it is meant to build. It is the same impact path (vaikutuspolku) that development grant applications ask for, and it is planned backwards, starting from the goal.

1 · Change

Start with the goal: the change you want to see in your students, defined as the EntreComp competences you target and the levels you aim for.

2 · Results

Then step back: what would show that change? In SkillsForge, a result is validated evidence that a student reached a level in a target competence, based on what they did, not on what they say.

3 · Activities

Then the work that produces those results: lessons, tasks and real-world challenges, observed by the teacher and defended to peers. Each activity writes evidence to known competences, so the link from activity to result is visible from the start.

Before the course, the logic model shows why the planned activities should lead to the goal. During it, it shows whether students are getting there and where the gaps are. At the end, the validated evidence shows what actually changed, ready for a development plan, a grant report or a municipal budget review.

The Skills Map

Twenty competences, four areas, eight levels of progression.

Each student's Skills Map is a live, visual portfolio of their evidence. Fifteen competences in three areas come from EntreComp, among them creativity, motivation and perseverance, working with others and learning through experience; the fourth area, emotions, is our extension based on Lackéus and is still being validated. A single task usually builds several competences at once, which is why this is a wheel rather than a tree — EntreComp itself states that no sequence is implied between competences. For school students, the first levels, Discover and Explore, are where the work usually starts, and the map always shows the next step rather than the distance to an expert.

What it is built on

Sources.

EntreComp — JRC, 2016

The European Commission's reference framework for entrepreneurship as a transversal competence: fifteen competences across three areas, on an eight-level progression. This is what evidence is mapped to.

Lackéus — OECD, 2015

Value creation pedagogy. The defining test is whether learners create value for people outside their own group. Competence develops whether or not the attempt succeeds, so a failure still produces evidence.

Schelfhout, Bruggeman & De Maeyer, 2016

Assessing competence through scaled behavioural indicators instead of Likert self-report. The direct methodological precedent for the rubrics.

Sarasvathy — effectuation

Supplies the novice-to-expert contrast that grades every indicator scale, because that contrast was derived from comparing expert entrepreneurs with novices rather than invented.

Lonka et al. — Microsoft, 2017

Road to 21st Century Competences: a framework, based on the Finnish national curriculum, for teachers to evaluate how well their lessons build the seven transversal competences, on levels from the teacher introducing a skill to students directing their own learning. A precedent for linking lessons to transversal skills, as SkillsForge's logic models do.

Lonka — Edita, 2018

Phenomenal Learning from Finland: Finland's path to 21st-century competences and phenomenon-based learning, in which students study real-world phenomena across subject boundaries. Background for building transversal skills through everyday lessons.

Obschonka, Hakkarainen, Lonka & Salmela-Aro, 2017

Frames entrepreneurship as a twenty-first century skill. In a two-wave study of 523 Helsinki high school students, entrepreneurial alertness and intention were linked to competences such as leadership, creativity and proactivity, the kind of competences EntreComp maps.

Ranta, Kruskopf, Kortesalmi, Kalmi & Lonka, 2022

A survey of 591 Finnish student teachers: high interest in teaching all seven transversal competences, but the least confidence and highest perceived cost for working life and entrepreneurship, alongside ICT. The gap SkillsForge's teacher training is meant to close.

What we do not claim. SkillsForge shows development, not destiny. It never labels a student, and no competence profile has been shown to predict later success. It won't raise PISA results by itself either. It shows whether the teaching is working, so schools can act.
