Theory of change

Every course gets its own theory of change

A theory of change shows how and why a course should lead to the change you want. SkillsForge scaffolds it as a logic model while the teacher plans, working backwards: first the change, then the results that will show it, and only then the activities. Each activity writes evidence to known competences, so the line from task to skill is visible from the start.

  1. 1

    Starting point

    What the students can already do, read from their Skills Maps.

  2. 2

    Activities

    Lessons, projects and peer defences that give room to practise the target skills.

  3. 3

    Outputs

    What the activities produce: student work, teacher observations and records.

  4. 4

    Outcomes

    Validated evidence that a student reached a level in a target competence.

  5. 5

    Impact

    The longer-term change the course aims at, for example students who take the initiative and keep going when work gets hard.

Every link in the chain names its assumptions, for example that there is time for project work, so everyone can see what has to hold for the course to work.

It is the same impact path (vaikutuspolku) that development grant applications ask for. Before the course, it shows why the activities should lead to the goal. After it, the validated evidence shows what actually changed, ready for a development plan, a grant report or a budget decision.

Three reference frameworks

EntreComp

The European Entrepreneurship Competence Framework: 15 competences in three areas and eight progression levels. The backbone of the Skills Map.

Osaaminen 2035

Skills 2035: the Finnish National Agency for Education's forecast of the skills that will matter most in 2035. A reference for choosing which skills a course targets.

PISA

The OECD's assessment of how 15-year-olds use what they know in reading, mathematics and science, with a new area in each cycle: creative thinking in 2022, learning in the digital world in 2025. Its questionnaire also asks about effort and perseverance. A reference for what tasks ask of students.

Evidence and validation

The evidence plan is ready as soon as the course is

When a teacher drafts a course, SkillsForge immediately describes what evidence each activity should produce and how it will be validated. The course is then analysed with synthetic data and with real users, and its impact is reported along the theory of change.

  1. AT ONCE

    Evidence plan

    For every target skill: which activity shows it, what counts as evidence, at which level, and how it will be validated.

  2. BEFORE THE COURSE

    Test with synthetic data

    Simulated classes with different starting points run through the plan. Gaps show up before any student is involved, for example a target skill that no activity can show.

  3. DURING THE COURSE

    Validate with real users

    Teachers and students use the course. Evidence appears on each Skills Map while the course runs, and a level counts only when a teacher has seen it in two different activities.

  4. AFTER THE COURSE

    Impact report

    The evidence is validated into a record of development, and the report follows the theory of change: what was done, what it produced, which levels were reached and what changed.

Synthetic data shows whether a course design can work. Only real use shows whether it did.

Calibrated people

Teachers who record evidence are trained and certified. The evidence is reliable because the people who record it are calibrated, not because of the software.

Gaps, not zeros

Missing evidence is shown as a gap, never as a zero. An attempt that did not work still produces evidence, so trying is never wasted.

Summaries for leaders

Principals and education directors see summaries across classes and schools during the school year, including which teaching approaches work.

Real data is analysed on the school's own server, like everything else in SkillsForge. Synthetic data is generated, so it contains no real students.

GDPR and trust

There is simply no data that leaves the school

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) requires a justification for transferring children's personal data. A closed perimeter covers most of these requirements through the deployment itself.

For parents

Nobody analyses a child's records from outside or passes them to third parties.

For the school

Control without a list of external services and a separate consent for each.

For the municipality

The solution also suits schools with the strictest data requirements.

We do not promise absolute security, because there is no such thing. We show a concrete mechanism: perimeter, roles and log.

Frequently asked questions

Questions from parents and school administrators

Is this really 100% safe?

We do not say “absolutely safe”, because no system is. We show a concrete mechanism: the data sits on the school's server, it is not sent out, every sign-in goes into the log, and every role has its own access.

Does it replace grades or rank students?

No. There is no total score and no ranking, and grades stay as they are. The Skills Map shows evidence, gaps and a next step.

What is synthetic data used for?

To test a course design before it reaches a class. Synthetic data is generated, not collected from students, so it contains no real students. It shows whether a design can work; only real use shows whether it did.

Can a child sign in from a home computer?

Yes, through the sign-in page with authorisation. The data still stays on the school's server. It does not matter where you sign in from, only where the information is stored.

How does the AI work without the internet?

The model runs locally, inside the school's perimeter. External AI services are not used for student data.

Where do updates come from?

We publish controlled versions and the school applies them itself. The platform does not need a permanent external connection to work.

What if the school has no server of its own?

The FEONA instance is deployed in an isolated environment dedicated to that school. The data stays separate and is not mixed with anyone else's.

Who owns the data if the school stops using the platform?

The school. The data sits on its server, so if use stops the data stays with the school, not with us.

How is this different from Google Classroom and similar cloud platforms?

Cloud services keep data on their own servers and make the decisions themselves. FEONA is deployed inside the school: the school controls the data, and this covers most of the GDPR requirements.
