FEONA — entrepreneurship track
Working prototype, in development

Twenty threads of competence, written by evidence rather than claimed on a form.

Each wedge is one competence thread. Each ring is one level, from the centre outwards. A cell fills only when a certified instructor has observed the behaviour at that level and an evidence artefact is attached. Where nothing has been observed, the cell stays open.

The twenty challenges

Every challenge has to create value for someone outside the cohort. Peers and instructors do not count as the recipient — that is the test the whole instrument rests on. One challenge writes evidence to several threads at once, so the wheel fills unevenly, which is the intended behaviour.

The current set reaches level 6. Levels 7 and 8 have no challenges written for them yet, and the wheel shows that as an empty outer band rather than hiding it.

What this instrument does not do

  • No aggregate score. There is no single number for a participant, at any point.
  • No ranking between participants.
  • No back-filling. Evidence at level 3 does not create evidence at levels 1 and 2.
  • Missing evidence is shown as missing, never as a zero.
  • It evidences development, not destiny. Nothing here says what a participant will go on to do.

Who assesses

  • The assessment of record is the certified instructor's observation against a scaled behavioural indicator.
  • The peer defence runs against a fixed grid and informs the observation. It does not replace it.
  • Emotions threads are recorded by the learner in their own words only. Nothing infers emotion from a face, a voice or behaviour.
  • Evidence on the entrepreneurship track carries a validity period. A runway figure from eighteen months ago evidences nothing about today.

Framework and sources

  • Bacigalupo, M., Kampylis, P., Punie, Y. & Van den Brande, G. (2016). EntreComp: The Entrepreneurship Competence Framework. JRC, Publications Office of the European Union. doi:10.2791/593884
  • Lackéus, M. (2015). Entrepreneurship in Education. OECD background paper — value creation for external stakeholders.
  • Schelfhout, W., Bruggeman, K. & De Maeyer, S. (2016). Studies in Educational Evaluation — scaled behavioural indicators in place of Likert self-report.
  • Sarasvathy, S. — effectuation; grades several of the indicator scales.
  • Nabi, G., Liñán, F., Fayolle, A., Krueger, N. & Walmsley, A. (2017). Academy of Management Learning & Education — 159 studies reviewed; impact measurement predominantly short-term and subjective.

EntreComp is a reference framework published by the European Commission's Joint Research Centre, not a standard and not a certification. FEONA reuses it under the Commission's reuse policy. Nothing here is endorsed by, approved by or produced in partnership with the European Commission. The fourth area, Emotions, is a FEONA extension drawn from Lackéus's action-reflection challenge categories; it is not part of EntreComp.
